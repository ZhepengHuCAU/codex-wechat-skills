# Chart Selection

Choose the smallest chart that proves the claim.

| Analytical question | Preferred chart |
|---|---|
| How has one measure changed over time? | Line chart |
| How do a few scenarios compare? | Bar chart |
| How did the demand structure change? | Stacked bars or stacked area |
| How do stocks-to-use and price move through time? | Bars plus line, clearly labeled dual axes |
| What is the historical relationship between two variables? | Scatter plot with a defensible fitted line |
| What contributed to a change? | Bridge/waterfall chart |
| How large is an announced increment under several denominators? | KPI comparison panel plus simple bars |

## Specific Practices

- Keep all observations when showing a long historical relationship, but fit the line only on the economically relevant sample when justified; disclose the sample.
- Use a green fitted line when the fitted sample points are green.
- When showing USDA balance-sheet demand, preserve USDA categories and split only exports by destination when reliable bilateral data are available.
- For “buffering” analysis, show the decline and offsets in the same unit and period. Report the replacement ratio as offset divided by the original decline.
- In a dual-axis card, match bar labels and the left axis color; match line labels and the right axis color. Avoid implying that differently scaled series have equal magnitudes.
